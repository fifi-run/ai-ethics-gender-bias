# The Ethical Implications of the Over-Sexualisation of Women in Artificial Intelligence

## Overview
This 2,937-word ethics essay examines the over-sexualisation of women in AI-generated imagery and connects the issue to dataset bias, platform incentives, algorithmic discrimination and unequal representation in AI development.

This is a critical, literature-based writing sample rather than an empirical or coding project.

## Central argument
The essay argues that AI systems can reproduce and amplify patterns of discrimination present in their training data and social environments. It challenges the idea that inspection of model internals alone is sufficient to solve algorithmic sexism, and emphasises representative data, institutional incentives and the social context in which automated systems are deployed.

## Structure and themes
1. How training data and design/optimisation choices shape generated outputs.
2. How biased datasets and proxy variables can encode gender stereotypes.
3. Consequences for career opportunity, mental health, privacy, deepfake abuse and unequal error rates.
4. Proposed responses, including representative datasets, reweighting, adversarial debiasing, fairness constraints and broader participation in AI research.
5. Why meaningful fairness requires institutional and societal change as well as technical interventions.

## Academic positioning
The essay complements technical projects by demonstrating critical engagement with responsible AI, dataset documentation, fairness evaluation and the social consequences of generative systems. It does not introduce a new dataset, train a model or conduct a fairness benchmark. Claims and examples are drawn from cited literature and should not be presented as original empirical findings.
